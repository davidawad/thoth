# Spritz patents (reference material)

Gathered 2026-10-06. This is reference gathering for Thoth's author, not legal advice. A patent attorney should be consulted before relying on any of it.

Scope: US patents and published applications about RSVP / word-at-a-time display / optimal recognition position (ORP) / display timing, assigned to Spritz Technology (Inc. / LLC) and its successor Spritz Holding LLC. Spritz Holding LLC (Park City, UT) is the assignee printed on the later patents. None of the documents names Maurice Ghanem or Robert Gold as an inventor; the inventors on every document below are Maik Steffen Maurer, Matthias Klein, Francis Abbott Waldman and Oleg Skrypnyuk (US20150143245A1 lists a further name on Google Patents that I did not confirm in the PDF).

How facts were verified: bibliographic data (numbers, titles, inventors, filing and grant dates, assignee, related applications, abstract, claims) was read from the downloaded PDFs themselves with `pdftotext` or, for image-only PDFs, OCR (`tesseract`). Legal status, assignment history and expiry come only from the Google Patents page (fetched through a summarizing fetch tool, not read raw) and are not confirmed against USPTO Patent Center / PAIR. Status is as shown by Google Patents on 2026-10-06; Google itself states legal status is an assumption, not a legal conclusion.

## Per-patent notes

Only US 8,903,174 and its continuation applications (US 2017/0229101, 2019/0325848, 2020/0193939, 2021/0312890) are about how a single word is aligned and timed. The rest cover where RSVP is embedded (notifications, lists, video, maps, augmented reality) and usage tracking, and mostly use the fixed-location display only as a limiting feature.

### US 8,903,174 B2 - Serial text display for optimal recognition apparatus and method

- Inventors: Maik Steffen Maurer, Matthias Klein, Francis Abbott Waldman. Assignee printed on the patent: Spritz Technology, Inc., Stoneham, MA. Appl. 13/547,982, filed 2012-07-12, granted 2014-12-02. 62 claims, 9 drawing sheets. Priority date shown by Google: 2012-07-12.
- Abstract: "Various embodiments are disclosed that relate to serially displaying text on an electronic display using techniques for placement of an optimal recognition position of words at a fixed display location. In some embodiments, an optimal recognition position character is displayed at the fixed display location. In other embodiments, an optimal recognition proportionate position is displayed at the fixed display location. Various related techniques for processing and displaying text are further disclosed herein."
- Independent claims: 1 (method of displaying), 19 (method of processing text data), 20 (computer program product), 47 (method of processing text data). Claim 1: serially displaying display elements "such that an optimal recognition position of at least some display elements ... is displayed at a substantially same location on the electronic display, referred to as a fixed display location, wherein, the at least some display elements are displayed such that the fixed display location is off-center toward the beginning of a displayed display element from the perspective of a reader". Claim 20 limits this to display elements "having more than four characters".
- Dependent claims that matter for an RSVP reader: how the position is chosen. Claim 5: the position is "a character within a display element, referred to as an optimal recognition position character" shown at the fixed location. Claims 6-9: by total characters in the element, 3-5 chars -> 2nd character; 6-9 -> 3rd; 10-13 -> 4th; 14-20 -> 5th. Claims 10-12, 14-17: alternatively a pixel offset equal to a proportion of the element's pixel width, "equal to the empirically determined recognition position ratio added to a quotient of 0.5 divided by the number of characters"; claim 14: average ratio 0.265; claim 15: offset between 0.20 and 0.45 of element width.
- Timing and context: claims 38-41 (and 44-46): a blank display element between sentences whose display time depends on the number of words in the previous sentence, a longer time above a first threshold and longer again above a second (claim 41: twelve and twenty-two words). The description says word display time is scaled with a multiplier by word length (FIG. 5a) and says longer words and longer sentences warrant longer display; the multiplier values are in a drawing that I did not extract.
- Other claimed details: claim 18 and 62, a word over thirteen characters is split into a first portion plus a hyphen and a second portion, shown sequentially; claims 32-37, a visual aid marking the fixed location (vertical lines above and below, or a different font colour for the character at the fixed location). The description also mentions a red font for the ORP character, speed ramp-up from a lower initial speed, and a progress slider and WPM indicator.
- Not verified: the FIG. 5a/5b multiplier values, and whether any claim was amended or disclaimed after grant.

## How this relates to Thoth

Thoth files read: `src/components/Reader/Reader.tsx` (`timingBelt`, `hyphenate`, `loop`), `src/components/TextParsingTools.ts` (`wordDifficultyMultiplier`), `src/components/DisplayReel.ts`, `src/components/PlaybackHead/PlaybackHead.tsx`, `src/components/Reader/Reader.css`, `src/components/constants.ts`. This section describes what the code does next to what the patent text says. It draws no conclusion about infringement, validity or legality.

Focus letter

- Thoth: `timingBelt` starts at the middle index `((len - 1) / 2) | 0`, then walks left to the first lowercase vowel (`/[aeiou]/`); if none, it keeps the middle index. There is no length table. `PlaybackHead` renders text before the focus letter, the focus letter in `<span className="red">`, and text after it.
- Patent (US 8,903,174 claims 5-9): the focus character is chosen by total character count (2nd for 3-5, 3rd for 6-9, 4th for 10-13, 5th for 14-20), or by a proportional pixel offset (0.265 ratio). For "present" (7 letters) Thoth's rule gives index 2 (the 3rd letter), which matches the patent table for that word; for other words the two rules differ (for example, a word with no vowel left of centre keeps the middle letter).
- The patent's description mentions a red font for the focus character; Thoth uses `color: red`.

Fixed display location

- Patent: the focus position is shown at "a substantially same location" on screen, "off-center toward the beginning" of the element.
- Thoth: `PlaybackHead` adds `MAX_DISPLAY_SIZE - hotCharInd` leading whitespace characters (`Array(n).join(...)` yields n-1 characters) inside `.Reader-canvas`, a fixed 21rem box with `text-align: center`. A comment in `Reader.css` states that count-based padding only gives a fixed width in a monospace font and that the app now uses a proportional font. I did not measure whether the focus letter lands on the same pixel column from word to word, so I cannot say whether Thoth holds it fixed.
- Thoth's guide marks: I saw no vertical guide lines (patent claims 32-37 mention them) in the CSS I read, only the red letter; other guide UI elsewhere in the app was not checked.

Per-word display time

- Thoth: base `60000 / wpm` ms; +25% if the word is longer than 6 characters; +50% if it contains a comma; +150% if it contains `.`, `?` or `!`; then, for words that start with an uppercase letter and are longer than 2 characters, multiplied by `wordDifficultyMultiplier` (1 + 0.15 per syllable beyond the first + 0.5 if not on the Dale-Chall list + 0.35 more if also not on the Spache list, capped at 3). Constants are in `constants.ts`.
- Patent (US 8,903,174): time multiplier as a function of word length (values in FIG. 5a, not extracted), plus a separate blank element after each sentence whose duration depends on that sentence's word count (claims 38-41). Thoth has no blank element and its sentence-end delay does not depend on sentence length; its timing also uses syllable count and dictionary familiarity, which I did not see in the claims I read.

Long words and display span

- Thoth: `timingBelt` hyphenates when `len > 14` or `len - focus > 7`; `hyphenate` splits words of 8 to 10 characters before their last three letters, and longer words after 7 characters (recursively), inserting "- ".
- Patent: claims 18 and 62 split words over thirteen characters into a first part with a hyphen and a second part, shown sequentially. The later US patents recite display elements that fit "within a perceptual span of 20 characters or less". Thoth shows one word or word fragment per frame.

Features of the other patents

- Notification markers, list-and-detail RSVP areas, video, map and AR integration, and consumption tracking (US 9,483,109, 9,632,661, 10,332,313, 10,712,916, 10,983,667, 11,644,944, 9,552,596): I found no matching feature in the files listed above. Thoth does show a progress count and a statistics block, but I did not compare these with the tracking claims.
